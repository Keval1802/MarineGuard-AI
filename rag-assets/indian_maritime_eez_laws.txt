INDIAN MARITIME LAW, EEZ REGULATIONS & COASTAL PROTECTION ACTS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Section 1: Indian Merchant Shipping Act & MARPOL Annex I/V Compliance
1. Territorial Water Discharge Limits: Under the Indian Merchant Shipping Act and MARPOL Annex I (Prevention of Pollution by Oil), no vessel shall discharge oily mixtures, tank washings, or engine room bilge water within 12 nautical miles of the Indian baseline, including the Gulf of Khambhat and Tapi Estuary.
2. Garbage & Plastic Discharge (MARPOL Annex V): Discharge of all plastics, synthetic ropes, and solid floating wastes into sea waters is strictly prohibited under international maritime law. Violations detected by remote sensing imagery or citizen reports trigger mandatory investigation by the Indian Coast Guard and Mercantile Marine Department (MMD).

Section 2: Indian Coast Guard Act 1978 & Emergency Powers
1. Response Authority: The Indian Coast Guard (ICG) is the designated Central Coordinating Authority for national oil spill disaster contingency planning (NOSDCP) in maritime zones of India.
2. Incident Classification:
   - Tier 1: Small localized spills (< 700 tonnes) handled by port authorities (Gujarat Maritime Board / Hazira Port).
   - Tier 2: Medium spills (700 to 10,000 tonnes) requiring regional Coast Guard activation.
   - Tier 3: Major disaster spills (> 10,000 tonnes) requiring national multi-agency mobilization.

Section 3: Candidate Source Attribution Disclaimer Protocol
1. Non-Accusatory Rule: Operational remote sensing evidence, SAR dark patches, and AIS proximity tracks constitute geospatial intelligence for site verification. Findings must be referenced as "candidate source" or "possible origin zone" to prevent premature legal attribution prior to physical sampling.
